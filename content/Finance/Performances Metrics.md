## Alpha in Finance

**Alpha** measures the **excess return, la surperformance** of an investment relative to a benchmark index (e.g., S&P 500). It represents the value added (or lost) by active management compared to passive market performance.

**Formula:**
$$
\alpha = R_i - [R_f + \beta \times (R_m - R_f)]
$$

Where:
- $R_i$ = Actual return of the investment
- $R_f$ = Risk-free rate
- $\beta$ = Beta (systematic risk)
- $R_m$ = Market return
**Interpretation:**
- **Alpha > 0:** Outperformed the market (positive skill or advantage).
- **Alpha < 0:** Underperformed the market.
- **Alpha = 0:** Performance matches the expected return from CAPM.

**Link to Valuation:**
- Alpha is often used in **portfolio performance analysis**, not directly in company valuation.
- However, it reflects **managerial skill**, which can influence perceived risk and valuation multiples.
